1 · Background

Clinical decisions are collaborative

How medicine already works

Tumor boards, transplant committees, ICU rounds: specialists propose, challenge, and reconcile competing assessments, because individual judgment is fallible — biased, incompletely informed, specialty-bound.

What a single LLM reproduces

The weaknesses of a single decision-maker, amplified: hallucinated facts, premature conclusions, fluent but flawed reasoning — with no internal mechanism for independent challenge.

A distinct paradigm answers this: several LLM agents collaborating on one clinical task — exchanging, critiquing, and revising each other's outputs before committing to a result.

1 · The paradigm

One inclusion rule does the heavy lifting: genuine inter-agent conditioning

Included

≥ 2 distinct LLM agents on a shared healthcare task, where at least one agent's output is revised, critiqued, or reconciled in response to another — feedback, debate, consensus, or conflict resolution, in the manner of MDT coordination.

Excluded

Single-LLM systems (even with tools or retrieval) · fixed sequential role-prompt chains without feedback · pre-LLM multi-agent systems · reviews and editorials without an implemented system.

Early exemplars

MedAgents — expert panels for zero-shot medical reasoning (Tang 2024)
MDAgents — collaboration structure adapts to case difficulty (Kim 2024)
Multi-agent conversations — mitigating clinicians' cognitive biases (Ke 2024)

Interaction protocols deliberately mirror case conferences, MDT meetings, and peer review.

Near-total proprietary reliance

Of the 50 studies naming any model, 49 name at least one proprietary model; exactly one is open-weight-only.

Why that compounds

Every agent turn is an API call. Multi-agent designs multiply cost, latency, and data-governance stakes relative to single-model deployment.

Shared infrastructure is emerging

CrewAI in 6 studies spanning elderly care to sepsis; AutoGen in 3. Generic orchestration libraries — not bespoke clinical software — are carrying these systems into hospitals.

3 · Reported performance

Consistent gains, on the benchmark's terms

Against single-agent baselines

MedAgents 86.7 vs 80.6 zero-shot (Tang 2024) · 89.2 vs 77.4 on MedQA (Chen 2025) · 97.8 vs 52.9 vs single-modality tools (Wan 2025) · best in 7 of 10 benchmarks (MDAgents, Kim 2024)

Attributable to interaction

+5.49% F1 from cross-validation over an evidence tree (Peng 2025) · PubMedQA 0.729→0.772 (MedARC, Miao 2026) · +6.4 / +8.5 pts from layered debating (Zhao 2025)

Largest real-data evaluation

Liver-transplant committee on 8,412 registry patients: 92.0% one-year survival accuracy (95% CI 91.4–92.6), 98.2% contraindication detection — still retrospective (Hasjim 2026, Lancet Digital Health).

The overhead is real

Collaboration costs 4–12× processing time (Wan 2025) · 3.6× tokens and wall-clock (Mandal 2025) · 3,657 s vs 1,853 s vs majority voting (Ma 2026) — and one benchmark asks when the overhead pays off at all (Zhu 2026).

3 · The human evidence

Seven studies out of eighty-one

Clinician-facing

Socrates 2.0: 61-participant clinician feasibility (Held 2025) · qualitative acceptability, HIV & mental health support (Humphries 2026) · emergent-care workload study (Walji 2026)

With participants

20 elderly users, ages 54–83, adaptive cognitive training (Ferri-Molla 2025) · 10 students, trust & interaction burden (Walji 2025) · 18 participants, dialogue evaluation (MentalAgora, Lee 2024)

By proxy

Patient-facing benefit measured through simulated patients, expert panels, rubrics — no patient-reported outcome, QoL instrument, or clinical endpoint in any included study.

The paradigm's claimed advantages — bias mitigation through disagreement, error interception through critique — are precisely the properties that need real clinical conditions to be tested.

3 · Q5 — Risks

Failure lives between the agents

Interaction-level failure modes

Collusion: many-to-one adversarial consensus — attack success 1.00, until a verifier agent drops it to 0.00 (Bashir 2025) · unsafe-content propagation across agent networks (MedSentry, Chen 2025) · false consensus that suppresses a correct minority.

Structural mitigations

Dedicated verifier / safety-checker agents (5 studies) · tiered agentic oversight — best on 4 of 5 healthcare safety benchmarks (Kim 2025) · human-in-the-loop consensus (Li 2025) · retrieval grounding as the most common hallucination control.

3 · Reading the absences

What the literature doesn't say

Zero randomized controlled trials

No RCT and no mixed-methods study among the 81. The strongest clinical evidence is one retrospective cohort.

No patient outcomes

No patient-reported outcome measure, quality-of-life instrument, or clinical endpoint in patients exposed to any system.

No workload instruments

No standardized clinician-workload measure in any study; no documentation time, turnaround time, or staffing cost reported.

No governance vocabulary

Liability, regulatory compliance, reimbursement, post-deployment monitoring: absent from every charted source.

These are absences from this map — the field has no measured answer yet, which is exactly why the map matters.

4 · Future directions

Three moves, in order of urgency

1

Standardized comparison

Routine head-to-head against strong single-model baselines on the same tasks; isolate the marginal value of each mechanism — debate, critique, consensus — through ablation (MedAgentBoard, Zhu 2026).

2

Prospective clinical validation

Predefined clinical endpoints, clinician workload measures, and error audits of inter-agent transcripts — motivated by the transplant-committee result (Hasjim 2026) and early acceptability work (Held 2025).

3

Interaction-level safety & reporting standards

Disclose agent roles, interaction topologies, termination rules, oversight points. Interaction transcripts are the multi-agent analogue of model cards.

5 · Conclusion

Architecturally mature, clinically unproven

The premise that structured disagreement among role-specialized agents reproduces the error-correcting properties of clinical teamwork is now built — in 81 studies across three years — but not yet tested where it counts.

Validate

Move from benchmarks and retrospective cohorts to prospective, clinician- and patient-facing studies.

Compare

Standardize against simpler baselines before crediting the collaboration itself.

Govern

Design oversight for the interaction — collusion, propagation, false consensus — not just the single output.

Multi-agent systems could extend multidisciplinary judgment to where human specialist collaboration is scarce — augmenting, not replacing, the clinical teams they imitate.
